I spent several year designing office buildings for inner city sites. This is a game played by a simple set of rules: the object is to achieve the maximum nett lettable area for the site for the minimum cost. The constraints are those of regulation - planning, building, escape in fire, and so on. The best strategy turns out to be quite simple: get the staircase in the right place, and the rest follows. What is relevant is how sensitive the design can be to small changes in the location of the staircase. At critical points, a minor change can flip the design into a completely different configuration - an orchestrated suite of changes. There are analogies to be drawn in many fields, such as mathematics - chaos and complexity theories, and physics - transition points between states of matter, crystal formation in supersaturated solutions.
We can consider a model of evolution an orchestrated suites of changes, which at critical stages may produce large morphological changes as a result of small genetic changes. Such transitions cannot be detected using cladistics as a method, because the underlying model of evolution implicit in the method cannot accommodate it. They key to understanding how some evolutionary changes happen may be to identify the critical element - by analogy, the location of the staircase - which orchestrates the other changes.
