Writing a scientific paper is a very creative process - as creative as designing a building. When I was studying architecture I learned that the most effective use of a six week design programme was to immerse myself in the problem for the first four weeks, studying every aspect, thinking about it all the time. For the next week go to Charmouth, collect fossils, drink a few beers, and not think about the project at all, then come back and spend the last week drawing everything up. I'd find that most problems were resolved, and so long as I didn't sleep too much, the projects were completed in time. Problems solving in this way is building the consistent and coherent model in the subconscious. It is an aspect of the human genius for pattern recognition. It allows us to recognise faces from very limited information by analysing information using our internal models of faces, and mapping data onto them. We can recognise people using extraordinarily small amounts of data.
The face is recognisable well before we have as few as 60,000 bits of data
