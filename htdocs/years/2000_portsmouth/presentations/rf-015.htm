One effect of this rigid body is to reduce mobility. Fishes, and presumably ichthyosaurs achieve much of their propulsion by movements of the body rather than the fins, and can flex the body to change direction quickly. A rigid body cannot do this, which is a problem in an animal hunting active, agile prey.

The skulls of plesiosaurs show them to have an exclusively carnivorous habit. The dentition, the height of the back of the skull and the large temporal fenestrae are all indicative of a hunter which relies on a fast and powerful bite to capture and immobilise prey. A detail of the jaw articulation may be a 'trigger' mechanism, allowing a fast snapping action. In evolutionary terms they have invested a lot in being predators. The length of the neck therefore becomes a necessity if agile prey is to be pursued, the rapid sideways movement of the head eliminating the necessity of rapid changes of direction of the body.
