The first, and most obvious, is that cladistics is not objective. The subjective element is hidden in most papers in the appendices listing the characters and character states. In defining a character, the author may be taking a feature which varies along a continuum between individuals in a sample, and setting arbitrary limits to define different states. Characters may be described as 'primitive' or 'secondarily derived' - terms implying a phylogeny. Weights of characters can be set to give the desired result. It is tacitly admitted by many authors that when presenting a cladogram in a paper, the input is 'tweaked' to give the 'right' result - the one which most closely matches their gut feeling - their internal model. There is nothing wrong with this - provided the author is explicit in reasoning why this should be the 'right' result.
Science is not objective . . but the subjective element must be explicit.
